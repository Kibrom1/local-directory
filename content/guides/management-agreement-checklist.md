# What to look for in a property management agreement

A management agreement is the document that turns a company's marketing into obligations. Before you sign, check four things: what the company will do, what it will charge, how your money is handled, and how you can leave. This page is a reading checklist for owners. It is not a template and it is not legal advice.

*General information, not legal advice. For questions about a specific contract, a qualified professional can review it. Last reviewed 2026-11-12.*

## Start with the state's short list

The Ohio Department of Commerce suggests owners get a signed copy of the agreement and confirm four things: all fees, including charges for additional services; how repairs are approved and paid; that the agreement requires monthly accounting reports; and how the agreement can be ended ([Ohio Department of Commerce landlord guide](https://com.ohio.gov/divisions-and-programs/real-estate-and-professional-licensing/consumers/landlords-property-management-guide)). The rest of this page expands on those items.

## 1. Services included

List what the manager will do and compare it with what you expect. Look for whether the agreement covers:

- Advertising, showings, screening and lease preparation
- Rent collection and late-rent follow-up
- Maintenance coordination and vendor payment
- Move-in and move-out handling
- Eviction filings, if needed, and who pays for them
- Inspections and how often they happen

If something you expect is not in the document, ask for it to be added or priced.

## 2. Fees

Compare every charge in the agreement against what you were quoted. Fee terms that companies publish on their own sites show how many parts a fee can have:

- **Greater-of formulas.** Reafco Ohio Elite Management's page lists a monthly fee of the greater of 9% of gross rents or $75 per unit ([Reafco](https://sites.google.com/view/ohio-elite-management/fees/monthly-management-fee)).
- **Plan-based amounts.** Swiss Property Management Group lists a Core and a Signature plan with different percentages and different dollar minimums for leasing and renewal ([Swiss PMG](https://www.swisspmg.com/pricing)). 10X Property Management lists several plans and also separate standalone prices ([10X](https://www.10xpropertymanagers.com/pricing)).
- **Minimums on one-time fees.** Evernest's Columbus page lists a $500 minimum on its leasing fee ([Evernest](https://www.columbuspropertymanagement.co/pricing)).
- **Per-home or per-unit basis.** Mynd's Columbus page lists tiered monthly amounts but does not say whether they are per home or in total ([Mynd](https://mynd.co/locations/ohio/columbus-property-management)).

These examples come from companies we reviewed and are not recommendations. They are here to show what to look for. In your own agreement, confirm:

- What the percentage applies to (rent collected or rent billed)
- Any minimum monthly amount
- Leasing and renewal fees, and whether a dollar floor applies (see [leasing fees versus renewal fees](/guides/leasing-fee-vs-renewal-fee/))
- Setup, onboarding, inspection, or markup charges on repairs
- Any charge when the agreement ends

## 3. Money handling and reports

The Commerce guide suggests asking whether rent and security deposits are held in a separate trust account, and how they are tracked and reported. It also suggests confirming the agreement requires monthly accounting reports, and reviewing statements and expense invoices when they arrive ([Ohio Department of Commerce landlord guide](https://com.ohio.gov/divisions-and-programs/real-estate-and-professional-licensing/consumers/landlords-property-management-guide)). Look for:

- When rent is paid out to you after it is collected
- What a statement shows and how you can get copies of invoices
- Who holds the security deposit and where

Ohio's landlord-tenant chapter defines "landlord" to include an agent authorized to manage the property or collect rent ([Ohio Revised Code 5321.01](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.01)), so you will want to know in writing who handles deposits and the related paperwork. See [how security deposits are handled in Ohio](/guides/ohio-security-deposits-process/).

## 4. Repairs and spending limits

The state's guide asks owners to confirm how repairs are approved and paid. Check for:

- A dollar amount above which the manager must ask you first
- Whether the manager uses its own vendors, and whether it discloses any ownership interest in them (a point the Commerce guide lists among its red flags)
- Whether you may use your own contractor

## 5. Term and ending the agreement

Look for the length of the term, whether it renews automatically, how much notice you must give, and whether any fee applies if you leave early or if a tenant the manager placed remains in the home. The Commerce guide says to confirm how the agreement can be ended, so make sure it is spelled out.

## What to check before you sign

- You have a complete signed copy
- Every fee in the agreement matches your written quote
- Services you expect are named
- A spending limit and approval process for repairs
- Monthly statements and access to invoices
- Where rent and deposits are held
- Notice period and charges for ending the agreement
