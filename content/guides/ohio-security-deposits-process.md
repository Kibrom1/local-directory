# How security deposits are handled under Ohio law: a process overview

Ohio's security deposit rules are in a single statute, Ohio Revised Code section 5321.16. In short, the statute covers three things: interest on larger deposits, an itemized written notice for any deduction, and a 30-day window to return what is owed. This page describes the process in plain language and links to the primary source. Read the statute itself before acting on anything here.

**This is general information and not legal advice.** It describes what the statute says on the date shown and may not cover your situation. The primary source is [Ohio Revised Code 5321.16](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.16). For questions about your own deposit, consult the statute directly or a qualified professional.

## What counts as a security deposit

Ohio's landlord-tenant chapter defines a security deposit as money or property a tenant gives to secure the tenant's obligations under a rental agreement ([Ohio Revised Code 5321.01](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.01)). The same section defines "landlord" to include an owner or someone acting for the owner, such as an agent authorized to manage the property or collect rent. In practice, when a property manager holds the deposit, the statute's landlord obligations are relevant to how that manager handles it.

## The process, in order

### 1. Interest on part of the deposit

Under subsection (A), only the part of a deposit that exceeds the greater of $50 or one month's periodic rent earns interest. The rate stated in the statute is 5% per year, and it applies if the tenant remains in the premises for six months or more. The landlord computes the interest and pays it to the tenant every year ([ORC 5321.16(A)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.16)).

### 2. The tenancy ends

The next step is triggered when the rental agreement ends and the tenant delivers possession. Subsection (B) lets the landlord apply the deposit to unpaid rent and to losses caused by the tenant's violation of the lease or of section 5321.05 ([ORC 5321.16(B)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.16)).

### 3. Itemized written notice of deductions

If the landlord deducts anything, the deductions must be itemized and identified in a written notice. The notice, together with any money still owed to the tenant, is due within thirty days after the agreement ends and possession is delivered ([ORC 5321.16(B)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.16)).

### 4. The forwarding address

Subsection (B) also requires the tenant to give the landlord a written forwarding or new address for the notice. A tenant who does not provide one is not entitled to the damages and attorney fees described in subsection (C) ([ORC 5321.16(B)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.16)).

### 5. If the rules are not followed

Subsection (C) provides that a landlord who does not comply with subsection (B) may owe the tenant the money and property due, plus damages equal to the amount wrongfully withheld, plus reasonable attorney fees ([ORC 5321.16(C)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.16)).

## A simple timeline

| Stage | What the statute describes |
|---|---|
| During the tenancy | Interest on the part of the deposit above the greater of $50 or one month's rent, if the tenant stays six months or more |
| Tenancy ends, possession delivered | Deposit may be applied to unpaid rent and losses |
| Within 30 days | Itemized written notice of deductions and any balance reach the tenant |
| Tenant's responsibility | Written forwarding address supplied to the landlord |
| If rules are not met | Remedies in subsection (C) |

## What to check as a landlord

- Where the deposit is held, and who holds it if you use a manager
- Whether the deposit is large enough that interest applies
- Whether you have a record of the date possession was returned, since the 30-day period is counted from it
- Whether you can produce an itemized list of every deduction
- Whether you have the tenant's written forwarding address

If you use a manager, the Ohio Department of Commerce suggests asking how the manager holds and reports security deposits, including whether they go into a separate trust account ([Ohio Department of Commerce landlord guide](https://com.ohio.gov/divisions-and-programs/real-estate-and-professional-licensing/consumers/landlords-property-management-guide)).
