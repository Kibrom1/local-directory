# How to choose a property manager in Columbus, Ohio

Choosing a manager comes down to three things: whether the company is licensed to do the work, how it handles your money, and whether its fees and duties are written down clearly. This guide lists the questions to ask and shows where the state of Ohio lets you check a license. It does not rank or recommend any company.

*General information, not legal advice. Last reviewed 2026-11-12.*

## Step 1: Confirm the license

Ohio has no separate property manager license. The Division of Real Estate and Professional Licensing says that managing rental property for another person for a fee generally requires an Ohio real estate license, with salespersons working under a supervising broker. Owners managing their own property are among the exceptions ([Ohio Department of Commerce, property management](https://com.ohio.gov/divisions-and-programs/real-estate-and-professional-licensing/salespersons-and-brokers/guides-and-resources/property-management)). The state also says that whether a license is needed depends on the facts of each case ([Ohio Department of Commerce news release](https://com.ohio.gov/about-us/media-center/news/real-estate-division-reminds-consumers,-property-owners-to-brush-up-on-the-law-ahead-of-travel-season)).

In practice, ask the company for the name of its managing broker and its license number, then check it yourself.

### How to look up a license

1. Go to the state's License Lookup tool, which the Commerce landlord guide links for checking a manager's license: [Ohio eLicense license lookup](https://elicense.lpi.ohio.gov/s/licenseshome).
2. Search by the company or broker name, or by the license number the company gave you.
3. Look at the license type, whether it is active, and whether the name matches the company you are dealing with.
4. If you cannot find a record, do not assume the worst. Ohio REALTORS reported that the state moved to a new licensing system on 2025-10-20 and that the new lookup would not be fully current until licensees claimed their licenses ([Ohio REALTORS FAQ](https://www.ohiorealtors.org/blog/2314/faqs-about-the-new-lpi-system/)). Search again under a different spelling, then ask the company, or contact the Division at 614-466-4100 for help confirming a licensee ([Ohio Department of Commerce news release](https://com.ohio.gov/about-us/media-center/news/real-estate-division-reminds-consumers,-property-owners-to-brush-up-on-the-law-ahead-of-travel-season)).

The lookup site's layout and the lookup tool itself may change, so follow the current link from the Commerce page above if the address stops working. A company's website saying it is licensed is a statement by the company, not a state record, so confirm it in the lookup.

## Step 2: Ask about money handling

The Ohio Department of Commerce suggests owners ask whether rent and security deposits go into a separate trust account, and how the manager tracks and reports those funds ([Ohio Department of Commerce landlord guide](https://com.ohio.gov/divisions-and-programs/real-estate-and-professional-licensing/consumers/landlords-property-management-guide)). It also suggests asking what insurance the manager carries, including fidelity bonds and errors-and-omissions coverage, and confirming that your own policy covers fraud by a property manager.

## Step 3: Ask about operations

From the same state guide, questions to put to any company:

- The full legal name, business address and phone number
- How long it has operated, and how many properties and employees it manages
- How it screens tenants
- The average time it takes to fill a vacancy
- Its eviction history, including evictions in the last 12 months
- Which professional affiliations or certifications you can verify

## Step 4: Compare fees on equal terms

Ask each company for the same things in writing: the monthly fee and what it applies to, the leasing fee, the renewal fee, any minimums, and any extra charges. Our [fee comparison](/fees/) shows the prices nine Columbus-area companies publish on their own sites. These are companies we reviewed and not a market average, and a company that does not publish prices may still be a fit. See [what property management costs in Columbus](/guides/property-management-cost-columbus/) for how to read the numbers.

## Step 5: Read the agreement before signing

Get a signed copy, and confirm fees, repair approval, monthly reports and how the agreement can be ended. The detailed checklist is in [what to look for in a management agreement](/guides/management-agreement-checklist/).

## Warning signs listed by the state

The Commerce guide names several: refusing to meet in person or give business contact details, repeatedly late rent remittances or unexplained delays, failing to supply invoices or documentation for expenses, and not disclosing an ownership interest in repair companies used on your property ([Ohio Department of Commerce landlord guide](https://com.ohio.gov/divisions-and-programs/real-estate-and-professional-licensing/consumers/landlords-property-management-guide)).

## What to check before you decide

- License verified in the state lookup, not only on the company's site
- Separate trust account for rent and deposits
- Insurance and bonding described in writing
- A full list of fees, including leasing, renewal and any minimums
- A signed agreement with monthly statements and an exit clause
- Any related repair or vendor companies disclosed

## If something goes wrong

The Commerce guide says concerns about a licensed manager can be reported to the Division of Real Estate and Professional Licensing at 614-466-4100 or through its complaint form ([Ohio Department of Commerce landlord guide](https://com.ohio.gov/divisions-and-programs/real-estate-and-professional-licensing/consumers/landlords-property-management-guide)).
